Narrator: Today we are going to Athens with Paul. He stood in the middle of the Areopagus, the most famous meeting place in the city, and he spoke to people who believed in many gods.
Friend: That sounds like my workplace. My colleague says all religions are the same, and it does not matter what you believe. What should I say to her?
Narrator: Paul shows us how. He did not begin by arguing. He began by listening. He said: Men of Athens, I perceive that in every way you are very religious. For as I passed along and observed the objects of your worship, I found also an altar with this inscription: To the unknown god.
Friend: So he started with what they already believed?
Narrator: Exactly. What therefore you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you. Paul found common ground, and then he proclaimed the truth. The God who made the world and everything in it, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not live in temples made by man, nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything.
Friend: That is very different from what they believed. Did they listen?
Narrator: Some did, and some did not. Paul told them that God made from one man every nation of mankind, and that they should seek God, and perhaps feel their way toward him and find him. Yet he is actually not far from each one of us, for in him we live and move and have our being.
Friend: I love that line. In him we live and move and have our being.
Narrator: Paul even quoted their own poets: For we are indeed his offspring. Although many people in Athens worshipped many gods, Paul showed them the one true God. On the other hand, he never forced anyone. He reasoned, he persuaded, and he left the result to God.
Friend: So what should I do with my colleague?
Narrator: Peter gives us the rule: In your hearts honor Christ the Lord as holy, always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect. Be ready with a reason, and give it gently.
Friend: Then I will do this: I will listen to her first, I will find something we agree on, and I will tell her why my hope is in Christ.
Narrator: That is evangelism in a pluralist world. Respectful, clear, and gentle. And it leaves the result to God.
