Narrator: One day, Jesus went up on a mountainside and sat down. His disciples came to him, and he began to teach them. These words are called the Sermon on the Mount, and people have listened to them for two thousand years.
Narrator: Jesus said, Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted. Blessed are the meek, for they will inherit the earth. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled.
Narrator: Jesus also said, You are the salt of the earth. You are the light of the world. Let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.
Narrator: He taught his disciples to pray: Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us today our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors.
Narrator: And he said, Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth. Store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
Friend: That was beautiful. But I have a question. Jesus says, Blessed are the meek. What does meek mean? Is it the same as weak?
Narrator: Good question. No, meek does not mean weak. A meek person is gentle and patient. They do not fight for their own rights. They trust God to defend them. Moses was a meek man, and Jesus himself was meek, but nobody would call them weak.
Friend: I see. And what about blessed? Does it mean happy?
Narrator: It is deeper than happy. Blessed means God is pleased with you, and you have His favor. A blessed person may suffer, but they have peace, because God is with them.
Friend: I have already read the Beatitudes twice this week, and I still have so many questions. Have you ever memorized them?
Narrator: Not yet, but I have studied them for many years. Let us read them together again this evening. We can learn one verse each day.
