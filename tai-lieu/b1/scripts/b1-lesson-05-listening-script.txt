Narrator: Paul wrote his letter to the Romans to explain the heart of the gospel. In chapter three, he says something surprising. No one is justified by works of the law. We cannot earn God's favour by keeping the law, because all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. But now, the righteousness of God has been shown apart from the law, through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe.
Friend: What does justified mean? I have heard the word, but I am not sure.
Narrator: To be justified means to be declared righteous by God, as if you had never sinned. It is a gift of grace, not a wage for good works. God put forward Jesus as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith. If we were justified by works, grace would not be grace, and boasting would be possible. But Paul says boasting is excluded. We are saved by faith alone.
Friend: But what about Abraham? He lived long before the law. Was he saved by works?
Narrator: No. Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness. He trusted God's promise, and God counted his faith as righteousness. Abraham was justified by faith, four hundred years before the law was given. If Abraham had been justified by works, he would have something to boast about, but not before God.
Friend: So what does justification change in our lives?
Narrator: Everything. Paul says in chapter five, since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. Through him, we have access by faith into this grace in which we stand. We rejoice in hope of the glory of God, and hope does not put us to shame. While we were still sinners, Christ died for us. That is how much God loves us.
Friend: That gives me such peace. If I trusted in my own works, I would never know if I had done enough. But if I trust in Jesus, I can rest.
Narrator: Exactly. Faith alone, in Christ alone, by grace alone. That is the good news of Romans.
