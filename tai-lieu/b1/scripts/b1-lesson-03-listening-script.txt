Narrator: Isaiah lived about seven hundred years before Jesus was born. God called him to be a prophet, a man who speaks God's words to God's people. Isaiah gave the people of Israel many messages from God. Some of his words were about the future, and one of his greatest prophecies is in chapter fifty-three.
Narrator: Isaiah fifty-three describes a Servant of God who would suffer for other people. Listen to these words. He was wounded for our transgressions. He was crushed for our iniquities. The punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed. These words were written long before Jesus came, but Christians believe they describe Jesus on the cross. He was despised and rejected by men, yet he carried our sorrows and our grief.
Friend: That is amazing. So Jesus was punished, but it was for our sins, not his own?
Narrator: Exactly. Isaiah says, the Lord laid on him the iniquity of us all. Jesus took our place. He was pierced for us, and because he suffered, we can have peace with God.
Friend: What about chapter fifty-five? I have heard the words, Come, all you who are thirsty. What do they mean?
Narrator: In Isaiah fifty-five, God invites everyone to come to him. Come, all you who are thirsty, come to the waters. Come, buy wine and milk without money and without cost. God's invitation is free. We cannot buy forgiveness. It is given to us by grace.
Friend: So what should we do?
Narrator: Isaiah says, seek the Lord while he may be found. Call on him while he is near. Let the wicked forsake their ways, and let them return to the Lord, and he will have mercy on them. He will freely pardon. God is ready to welcome everyone who comes to him.
Friend: I love that invitation. I want to share it with my friend this week.
Narrator: That is a wonderful idea. Tell your friend, you are invited. Come to God, and he will give you living water, and rest for your soul.
