Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Used to / would for past habits (used to + verb for habits and states; would + verb only for repeated actions; did not use to for negatives); future perfect intro (will have + past participle with by + future time)

Structure 1: Used to + Verb — Past Habits and Past States We use used to + verb for a habit or a state in the past that is no longer true now: “I used to worry about money all the time.” (I worried then; I do not worry now.) — “We used to be very poor.” (We were poor then; we are not poor now.) The negative is did not use to (note: use, not used): “We did not use to have a car.” The question is Did you use to…?: “Did you use to worry about money?”

Structure 2: Would + Verb — Repeated Past Actions Only We use would + verb for a repeated action in the past: “Every Sunday, my father would count the money for the week.” — “Every month, my grandmother would save a little money.” But would is only for actions, never for states. We cannot say “I would be poor” or “I would worry about money” for a state — for states, use used to: “I used to be poor.” — “I used to worry about money.” Test: if you can add “every Sunday” to the action, would is possible; for a feeling or a state, only used to works.

Structure 3: Future Perfect Intro — Will Have + Past Participle The future perfect talks about something that will be finished before a time in the future.

Form: will have + past participle. It is almost always used with by + a future time: “By next year, I will have saved enough money for an emergency.” (The saving will be finished before next year.) — “By June, she will have paid all her debts.” — “By 2028, they will have helped their children through school.” Compare the three times: past habit (I used to worry), present change (now I save every month), future result (by next year, I will have saved enough). Together they tell the whole story of a changed life.

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Linking sounds + intrusive /r/: when a word ending in /ə/, /ɔː/ or /ɑː/ meets a word beginning with a vowel, add a linking /r/ sound (law (r) and order, idea (r) of it, more (r) and more, far (r) away, Asia (r) and Africa); consonant-to-vowel linking (pick it up, save up) and vowel-to-vowel linking with /w/ and /j/ (go on, my own)

In natural English, words do not stand alone — they link together. When a word ends in a consonant and the next word begins with a vowel, the consonant joins the next word: pick‿it up, save‿up, look‿at it. When a word ends in a vowel sound and the next begins with a vowel, English adds a tiny glide sound: /w/ after /uː/ and /əʊ/ (go‿on, two‿of them) and /j/ after /iː/ and /aɪ/ (my‿own, see‿it). The intrusive /r/: when a word ends in the vowel sounds /ə/, /ɔː/ or /ɑː/ and the next word begins with a vowel, speakers add an /r/ sound to link them — even though there is no letter 'r' in the spelling. This is called the intrusive /r/. law (r) and order — the law ends in /ɔː/, so we add /r/. idea (r) of it — idea ends in /ə/, so we add /r/. more (r) and more — more ends in /ɔː/. far (r) away — far ends in /ɑː/. Asia (r) and Africa — Asia ends in /ə/. Watch out: do NOT add /r/ after other vowel sounds. We say see‿it (with /j/), not see (r) it; go‿on (with /w/), not go (r) on. And do not add /r/ before a consonant: the law of God has no /r/ link. The intrusive /r/ only appears before a vowel. Practice: read these aloud twice, linking every word: “The law and order of God is good. The idea of it is simple: more and more people are learning contentment. God is never far away.”

Bài nghe

Nghe toàn bộ bài, rồi trả lời 6 câu hỏi bên dưới. Giọng đọc: Casper (người dẫn) và Aria (đối thoại).

Thời lượng: 2:28 · 392 từ · Xem transcript (TXT)

Câu hỏi nghe hiểu

Câu 1. 1. What did Jesus say about storing up treasures?

Đáp án: Do not store up treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and thieves steal; store up treasures in heaven instead.

Câu 2. 2. What did the Friend use to do every night?

Đáp án: She used to worry about money all the time; every night she would check her bank account and think about tomorrow.

Câu 3. 3. What does Jesus say about the birds of the air?

Đáp án: Look at the birds of the air: they do not store up food, but God feeds them, and we are more valuable than they are.

Câu 4. 4. What did Paul write about godliness and contentment?

Đáp án: Godliness with contentment is great gain; we brought nothing into the world and cannot take anything out, so if we have food and clothing we should be content.

Câu 5. 5. What three things does the Narrator say the Friend should do with her money?

Đáp án: Make a simple budget, save a little every month, and give a little to help others; be generous and do not be greedy.

Câu 6. 6. What will the Friend have done by next year and the year after that?

Đáp án: By next year she will have saved enough for an emergency, and by the year after that she will have helped her sister with her school fees.

Bài đọc

Lan used to worry about money all the time. Every night, she would check her bank account on her phone, and she would think about tomorrow. Would she have enough? Would she lose her job? She worked hard, but her money always seemed to run away. One Sunday, her pastor read the words of Jesus: 'Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven.' Lan listened carefully. Jesus also said, 'Do not be anxious about your life. Look at the birds of the air: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not of more value than they?' Lan thought about her grandmother, who had never been rich. Every month, her grandmother would save a little money, and she would give some of it to help her neighbours. 'God gives us our daily bread,' she used to say. 'Enough is enough.' Lan decided to change. She made a simple budget. She saved a little every month, and she gave a little to help others. She stopped checking her bank account every night. By next year, she will have saved enough money for an emergency, and she plans to help her younger sister with her school fees. Lan is learning contentment. She cannot serve both God and money, so she has chosen her Master. Money is a good servant, but it is a bad master. 'Do not be anxious about tomorrow,' Jesus said, 'for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble.'
