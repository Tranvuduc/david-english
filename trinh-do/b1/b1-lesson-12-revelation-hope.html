Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Capstone review: present simple/continuous, past simple/continuous/perfect, present perfect, future will/going to, future perfect (intro), conditionals 1-3 recap, passive recap.

This lesson adds no new grammar point. Instead, we review everything from Lessons 01–11 in one table. The question to ask about every sentence is: when does it happen, and is it finished? If you can answer those two questions, you can choose the right form.

Mixing tenses in one story: “John saw (past simple) a new heaven. The first heaven had passed (past perfect) away. Jesus has risen (present perfect), so we have (present simple) a living hope today. One day, God will wipe (future) away every tear.”

Practise telling Revelation 21 in four tenses: what John saw (past), what is true now (present perfect), what God will do (future), and what will already be finished by then (future perfect).

Watch out: (1) never use will after if in a Type 1 conditional — say “If we trust God, He will guard us.” (2) Present perfect needs has/have — “Jesus has risen.” (3) Future perfect needs a future time marker — “By the time Jesus returns, …” or “By then, …”.

Bảng ôn tập đầy đủ các thì và dạng nằm trong file PDF bài học.

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Capstone review of connected speech and intonation: linking (wipe_away, river_of life), weak forms (of, to, and, have), elision (next day, last time), question vs statement intonation, all with fresh examples from Revelation 21-22 and 1 Peter 1.

This is our capstone pronunciation lesson: we bring together everything from the course — linking (Lesson 02), weak forms (Lesson 03), elision (Lesson 06), and intonation (Lessons 04 and 08) — with fresh examples from Revelation and 1 Peter. Linking: when a word ends in a consonant and the next word begins with a vowel, link them as one sound. wipe_away sounds like “wipe-away” as one word; river_of life sounds like “river-uv-life”; God_is sounds like “God-iz”. Practise: “He will wipe_away every tear.” — “The river_of life flows from the throne.” Weak forms: small words are almost never stressed. Of becomes /əv/ (“river of life”, “tree of life”), to becomes /tə/ (“born again to a living hope”), and becomes /ən/ (“the Alpha and the Omega”), have becomes /əv/ (“we have been born again”). If you stress these words, the sentence sounds slow and strange; if you weaken them, the important words stand out. Elision: in fast speech, a sound disappears. Last time becomes “las_time”, next day becomes “nex_day”, first heaven becomes “firs_heaven”. Practise: “a salvation ready to be revealed in the las_time.” Intonation — questions vs statements: a yes/no question rises at the end, and a statement falls. “Will God wipe away every tear?” (rising) — “God will wipe away every tear.” (falling — a promise). A wh-question falls too: “What will the new heaven be like?” Listen for the rise on “would we have any hope?” and the fall on “We have a living hope.” Practice: read these aloud twice, linking the words and weakening the small words: “God will wipe_away every tear from our eyes. We have been born again to_a living hope. The river_of life flows from the throne_of God.”

Đáp án: Hold on to that hope and rejoice, because she knows how the story ends.

Bài đọc

A Living Hope for a New World. Nearly two thousand years ago, the apostle John was living in exile on the small island of Patmos. But one day, God gave him a wonderful vision. John saw a new heaven and a new earth, because the first heaven and the first earth had passed away. He saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down from heaven, prepared as a bride. Then he heard a loud voice from the throne: 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people. He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more.' Before John's vision, the apostle Peter had written about the same hope. Peter said that God has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Because Jesus has risen, our hope is alive. We have an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for us. By God's power, we are being guarded through faith for a salvation that will be revealed in the last time. Peter was honest: for a little while we may be grieved by various trials. Trials test our faith, and our faith is more precious than gold. But one day, the testing will end. In the new city, the river of the water of life will flow from the throne of God, and the tree of life will bear fruit every month. Night will be no more, and the Lord God will be our light. We will reign with him forever and ever. That is why we can rejoice today, even in trials. The story is not over yet, but we know how it ends: God will make all things new.

Nói

In pairs, tell your partner about your living hope (2 minutes each). Use at least one present perfect sentence ('God has...'), one future sentence ('One day, I will...' or 'I am going to...'), and one conditional ('If I trust God, ...'). Your partner listens and asks one follow-up question. Then swap roles.

Viết

Write a paragraph of 120-150 words titled 'My Living Hope'. Use at least one present perfect sentence, one future form (will or going to), one future perfect sentence ('By the time Jesus returns, ...'), and one conditional. Describe what God has done for you, what you are looking forward to, and how your hope changes your life today.

Câu 2. 2. What did John see coming down from heaven?
