Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Second conditional (If + past simple, would + base verb); wishes and hypotheticals

Form: If + past simple, would + base verb. "If we were justified by works, grace would not be grace." — "If Abraham had been justified by works, he would have something to boast about." Use were (not was) after if for all persons in formal English: "If I were still under the law…" ("If I was…" is common in speech, but were is correct in writing.)

Meaning: the second conditional talks about unreal or imaginary situations — things that are not true now, or that we are only imagining. Paul uses it to show why salvation cannot be by works: "If we were justified by works" — but we are not! We are justified by faith.

Compare the first conditional (real future): "If you believe, you will have peace" — with the second (imaginary): "If you believed, you would have peace."

Wishes — the same past-simple form after wish: I wish + past simple — a wish about the present that is not true: "I wish I had peace with God." (I do not have it yet.) — "I wish I trusted Christ more." — "I wish I were sure of my salvation."

Note: wish takes were too: "I wish I were free from fear."

Would in the result clause: the main clause always uses would (not will): "If we trusted in our works, we would never know if we had done enough."

Short forms in speech: "If I trusted Him, I'd have peace."

Watch out: never use would in the If-clause: say "If we were justified by works…", not "If we would be justified…". And never use will in the result: say "…grace would not be grace", not "…grace will not be grace".

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

/θ/ vs /ð/ in context (faith, think, through, truth vs father, this, that, with)

Both sounds are made the same way: put the tip of your tongue between your teeth and blow air out. The difference is voice: /θ/ is voiceless (no vibration — put your fingers on your throat, you feel nothing), and /ð/ is voiced (your throat vibrates). Vietnamese has neither sound, so learners often replace them with /t/ or /d/ — "tink" for think, "dis" for this. Do not do that: keep the tongue between the teeth! /θ/ — voiceless (no vibration): faith /feɪθ/, think /θɪŋk/, through /θruː/, truth /truːθ/, three /θriː/, thank /θæŋk/. Whisper the sound: it is just air. /ð/ — voiced (throat vibrates): father /ˈfɑːðər/, this /ðɪs/, that /ðæt/, with /wɪð/, they /ðeɪ/, brother /ˈbrʌðər/. Hum the sound: you can feel it in your throat. In sentence context: "I think this is the truth" — think /θ/, this /ð/, the /ð/, truth /θ/. Listen for the vibration: if your throat buzzes, it is /ð/; if it is only air, it is /θ/. Practice: read Romans 5:1 aloud twice, slowly, feeling the difference — "Therefore, since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." Faith /θ/, with /ð/, through /θ/.

Câu hỏi nghe hiểu

Câu 1. 1. According to Romans 3, why can no one be justified by works of the law?

Đáp án: Because all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; no one can keep the law perfectly.

Câu 2. 2. What does 'justified' mean?

Đáp án: To be declared righteous by God, as if you had never sinned — a gift of grace, not a wage for good works.

Câu 3. 3. How was Abraham justified, and when did he live in relation to the law?

Đáp án: By faith — he believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness. He lived about 400 years before the law was given.

Câu 4. 4. According to Romans 5:1-2, what do we have since we have been justified by faith?

Đáp án: We have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, and access by faith into this grace.

Câu 5. 5. What would happen if we were justified by works?

Đáp án: Grace would not be grace, and boasting would be possible. If we trusted in our own works, we would never know if we had done enough.

Bài đọc

Romans is Paul's greatest letter, and its heart is a question: how can a sinful person be right with a holy God? Paul's answer is clear: no one can earn God's favour by keeping the law. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. The law shows us our sin, but it cannot save us. If we were justified by works, grace would not be grace, and boasting would be possible. But God has made another way. The righteousness of God has been revealed apart from the law, through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. God put forward Jesus as a propitiation by His blood, to be received by faith. On the cross, Jesus took the punishment our sins deserved, so that God can be both just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. Paul proves this from Abraham. Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness. Abraham lived about four hundred years before the law was given, so he could not have been saved by keeping the law. He was saved by faith, and so are we. God counts faith as righteousness for everyone who trusts in Christ. Then Paul tells us what justification changes. Since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. We have access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we rejoice in hope of the glory of God. Hope does not put us to shame, because God's love has been poured into our hearts. And the greatest proof of that love is this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. If we trusted in our own works, we would never know if we had done enough. But because we trust in Jesus, we can rest in peace.

Đọc hiểu

Câu 1. 1. According to the passage, why can no one earn God's favour by keeping the law?

Đáp án: No one can be justified by works, because all have sinned and no one keeps the law perfectly.

Câu 2. 2. How was Abraham justified, and why does the timing matter?

Đáp án: He believed God's promise, and God counted his faith as righteousness — about 400 years before the law was given.

Câu 3. 3. What does it mean to be justified?

Đáp án: To be declared righteous by God, as if we had never sinned; it is a free gift of grace through faith in Christ.

Câu 4. 4. What four blessings does Romans 5 say belong to those who are justified by faith?

Đáp án: Peace with God, access into grace, hope of God's glory, and hope that does not put us to shame — even in suffering.
