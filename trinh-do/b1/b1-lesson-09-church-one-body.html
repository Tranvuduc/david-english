Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Gerunds and infinitives: verb + -ing (enjoy, love, stop + -ing) vs verb + to-infinitive (want, decide, hope, called us to love); verbs with meaning change: stop, remember

A gerund is a verb with -ing used as a noun: “Serving together is a joy.” An infinitive is to + verb: “God called us to love one another.” Some verbs are followed by a gerund, some by an infinitive — and the choice is fixed, so you must learn which verbs take which form.

Structure 1: Verb + Gerund (-ing) Use the gerund after these verbs: enjoy, love, hate, like, finish, practise, stop, start, keep, mind, suggest, avoid. Examples: “We enjoy serving together.” — “She loves encouraging her friends.” — “He stopped worrying about his gift.” After a preposition, always use the gerund: “Thank you for helping me.” — “We grow by serving together.”

Structure 2: Verb + to-Infinitive Use the infinitive after these verbs: want, decide, hope, plan, need, learn, choose, promise, try, remember, forget. Examples: “God called us to love one another.” — “I decided to help with the Sunday school.” — “They hope to grow up into maturity.” — “We want to build up the body of Christ.” The infinitive also shows purpose — why we do something: “Christ gave gifts to equip the saints.” — “We meet to worship God together.”

Structure 3: Verbs That Take Both — With a Change of Meaning Stop: stop + -ing means quit doing it: “I stopped worrying.” (I do not worry any more.) Stop + to + verb means stop in order to do something else: “We stopped to pray.” (We stopped our walk in order to pray.) Remember: remember + -ing means remember a past action: “I remember singing in the choir.” (I sang, and I remember it.) Remember + to + verb means do not forget to do something in the future: “Remember to pray for our pastors.” (Do not forget to pray.)

Watch out: never say “I enjoy to serve” (use serving) and never say “I want serving” (use to serve). A useful trick: learn the verb and its form together — enjoy serving, want to serve — as one pair, not as two separate words.

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Stress in compound nouns: stress falls on the first word (CHURCH building, SUNday school, TEAMwork, BIBLE study, PRAYER meeting); contrast with adjective + noun phrases (a NEW church, a BIG team)

A compound noun is two words joined to make one idea: church + building = church building, Sunday + school = Sunday school. In English, the stress almost always falls on the FIRST word of a compound noun. Vietnamese learners often stress the second word, which can confuse the listener. Learn the rule: CHURCH building — not church BUILDing. SUNday school — not Sunday SCHOOL. TEAMwork — not team WORK. BIBLE study — not Bible STUDy. PRAYER meeting — not prayer MEETing. WORship team — not worship TEAM. BODY of Christ — the word body carries the stress in this phrase too. Words ending in -ship: these are one word, and the stress falls on the first syllable: HEADship (not head SHIP), FRIENDship, LEADERship, MEMBERship. Say the first part louder and longer, and the second part quickly and quietly. Contrast — adjective + noun phrases: when a word describes a noun (not a compound), the stress falls on the noun: a NEW church, a BIG team, a GOOD teacher. Compare: “We meet in the CHURCH building” (compound — stress on CHURCH) and “We meet in a NEW church” (phrase — stress on the noun). The rule: compound noun, stress the first word; describing phrase, stress the noun. Practice: read these aloud twice, clapping on the stressed word: “I teach in the SUNday school. Our WORship team meets in the CHURCH building. TEAMwork helps the BODY of Christ to grow.”

Đáp án: She sings in the choir, but she thinks her gift is not important because other people preach and teach.

Câu 3. 3. What gifts does the Narrator name from 1 Corinthians 12?

Đáp án: Apostles, prophets, teachers, serving, and encouraging others.

Câu 4. 4. According to Ephesians 4, what must the church do to grow up into maturity?

Đáp án: Use its gifts to build up the body, keep unity in the faith, and speak the truth in love.

Câu 5. 5. What did the Friend decide to do this year?

Đáp án: To help with the Sunday school, and to serve with joy.

Câu 6. 6. What does the Friend want to do for her friends every morning?

Đáp án: She wants to pray for them and to speak words that build them up.

Bài đọc

The church is not a building. The church is a body — the body of Christ. In 1 Corinthians 12, Paul says that just as a human body has many members, so it is with Christ. The eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of you." The head cannot say to the feet, "I have no need of you." Every member is needed, and every member is important. God has given different gifts to different people. Some are apostles, some are prophets, some are teachers. Some have the gift of serving, and some have the gift of encouraging others. No gift is small, and no member is unimportant. If one member suffers, all suffer together. If one member is honoured, all rejoice together. In Ephesians 4, Paul writes that Christ gave these gifts to His people to build up the body, until we all reach unity in the faith and grow up into maturity. We must learn to speak the truth in love, so that the whole body grows. When each member does its part, the body builds itself up in love. There is one body and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and one God and Father of all. That is the unity we must keep. We enjoy serving together, and we want to grow together. God called us to love one another, to encourage one another, and to build one another up. That is what it means to be the church — one body, many members, working together in love.

Đọc hiểu

Câu 1. 1. Why does Paul compare the church to a body?

Đáp án: Because, like a body, the church has many different members, and every member is needed and important.

Câu 2. 2. According to 1 Corinthians 12, what happens when one member suffers?

Đáp án: All the members suffer together with it.

Câu 3. 3. According to Ephesians 4, why did Christ give gifts to His people?

Đáp án: To equip the saints and to build up the body of Christ, until we all reach unity in the faith and grow up into maturity.

Câu 4. 4. What does it mean to "speak the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15)?

Đáp án: It means we tell each other the truth in a loving way, so that the whole body grows up into Christ.

Câu 5. 5. How many bodies, Spirits, Lords, and faiths does Paul name in Ephesians 4:4-6?

Đáp án: One of each: one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all.
