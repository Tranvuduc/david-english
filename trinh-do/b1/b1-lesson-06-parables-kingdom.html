Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Reported speech: statements (say/tell + that-clause, tense backshift), questions (ask + if/whether/wh-word), commands (tell/ask + to-infinitive)

When we tell someone what another person said, we usually shift the tense back one step — because the words were spoken in the past. This is called reported speech (or indirect speech).

Structure 1: Reported Statements — say / tell + that Direct: Jesus said, “The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed.” Reported: Jesus said that the kingdom of heaven was like a mustard seed. (In Bible retelling, the present tense is often kept when the truth is still true: “Jesus said that the kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed” is also correct.) Use say with no person, and tell with a person: “He said that he would return.” — “He told his servants that he would return.” Never say “He said me that…” Backshift table — direct speech to reported speech: Present simple to past simple: “The seed is the word of God.” to “The teacher said that the seed was the word of God.”

Present continuous to past continuous: “The thorns are growing.” to “He said that the thorns were growing.” Will to would: “I will return.” to “The master said that he would return.” Can to could: “I can explain it.” to “Hoa said that she could explain it.” Present perfect to past perfect: “I have found the treasure.” to “He said that he had found the treasure.”

Structure 2: Reported Questions — ask + if / whether / Wh-word Yes/No questions use if or whether, and the word order becomes statement order (no do / does / did, no question mark): Direct: “Is the kingdom like a pearl?” Reported: “Lan asked if the kingdom was like a pearl.” Wh-questions keep the question word: Direct: “What does the parable mean?” Reported: “The disciple asked what the parable meant.”

Structure 3: Reported Commands — tell / ask + person + to + verb Direct: “Watch and be ready.” Reported: “Jesus told the disciples to watch and be ready.” Direct: “Use my money well.” Reported: “The master told his servants to use his money well.” For a negative command, use not to: “Do not be afraid” to “He told them not to be afraid.”

Watch out: do not keep the question word order in reported questions — say “He asked what the parable meant”, not “He asked what did the parable mean”. And remember: say takes no person (“He said that…”), tell always takes a person (“He told me that…”).

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Connected speech — elision: the /t/ and /d/ that disappear between consonants (next day, first time, must be, kind of)

In natural, connected English, sounds often disappear. When /t/ or /d/ sits between two consonants, native speakers usually drop it — the word is still understood from the context. Vietnamese learners often pronounce every sound carefully ("next day" with a clear /t/), which sounds slow and foreign. Learn to drop the sound: next day — the /t/ disappears: "neks day" /neks deɪ/. first time — the /t/ disappears: "firs time" /fɜːs taɪm/. must be — the /t/ disappears: "mus be" /mʌs biː/. kind of — the /d/ disappears: "kine of" /kaɪn əv/. last night — the /t/ disappears: "las night" /lɑːs naɪt/. old friend — the /d/ disappears: "ol friend" /əʊl frend/. Rule: the sound disappears only between consonants, and only in fast, natural speech. In slow, careful speech — reading the Bible aloud in church, for example — you may keep the sound. Never drop a sound at the start of a word, and never drop /t/ or /d/ before a vowel: say "next hour" with the /t/ (next + vowel), but "next day" without it (next + consonant). Practice: listen to the lesson audio and shadow it — copy the elision exactly. Then read these sentences aloud twice: once slow and careful (all sounds), once natural (with elision): "The first time I heard the parable, I must be honest — it was kind of hard to understand."

Bài nghe

Nghe toàn bộ bài, rồi trả lời 6 câu hỏi bên dưới. Giọng đọc: Casper (người dẫn) và Aria (đối thoại).

Thời lượng: 2:22 · 383 từ · Xem transcript (TXT)

Câu hỏi nghe hiểu

Câu 1. 1. How did Jesus often teach the people?

Đáp án: With parables — short stories that show what the kingdom of heaven is like.

Câu 2. 2. In the parable of the sower, which seed gave a harvest?

Đáp án: Some seed fell on good soil and gave a harvest — a hundred, sixty, or thirty times as much.

Câu 3. 3. What did Jesus say the seed and the soil mean?

Đáp án: The seed is the word of God, and the soil is the heart of the person who hears it.

Câu 4. 4. What did Jesus say the kingdom of heaven is like?

Đáp án: Like a grain of mustard seed — the smallest seed, but it grows into the largest garden plant.

Câu 5. 5. What two things did Jesus say the kingdom is like, and what did the men do?

Đáp án: Like treasure hidden in a field, and like a merchant who found one pearl of great value — both sold everything to have it.

Câu 6. 6. What did the master in the parable of the talents say and ask?

Đáp án: He said that he would return, and he asked his servants to use his money well. The faithful servants doubled what they had been given.

Bài đọc

Jesus loved to teach with parables — short, simple stories that carry a deep truth about the kingdom of heaven. He told His disciples that the kingdom is like a sower who went out to sow his seed. Some seed fell along the path, and the birds ate it. Some fell on rocky ground, where it grew quickly but had no root, so the sun burned it. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns choked it. But some seed fell on good soil, and it gave a rich harvest — a hundred, sixty, or thirty times as much as was sown. The seed is the word of God, and the soil is the heart that hears it. Then Jesus said that the kingdom is like a grain of mustard seed. It is the smallest of all seeds, yet when it grows, it becomes the largest of all garden plants, and the birds come and rest in its branches. The kingdom starts small, but it grows very great. He also said that the kingdom is like treasure hidden in a field. When a man found it, he sold all that he had and bought that field. It is also like a merchant who found one pearl of great value, and sold everything to buy it. The kingdom is worth everything we have. In Matthew 25, Jesus told two more parables about being ready. Ten virgins waited for the bridegroom, but only five were wise and took oil for their lamps. And a master gave his servants talents and asked them to use his money well. The faithful servants were rewarded, and the master said, 'Well done, good and faithful servant.' The parables ask us a question: what kind of soil are we, and are we ready for the King?

Viết

Write a short retelling (80-100 words) of one parable from Matthew 13 or 25, in reported speech. Begin: 'Jesus told his disciples a parable. He said that...' Use at least 4 reported-speech sentences (said that / told ... to ... / asked ...) and 5 vocabulary words from Section 3.

Đọc hiểu

Câu 1. 1. Why did Jesus teach in parables?

Đáp án: He teaches with short stories (parables) that show what the kingdom of heaven is like, so that ordinary people can understand deep truths.

Câu 2. 2. What does the mustard seed teach us about the kingdom of heaven?

Đáp án: It grows from the smallest seed into the largest garden plant — the kingdom starts small but grows very great.

Câu 3. 3. What did the man who found the treasure and the merchant who found the pearl have in common?

Đáp án: Both the man and the merchant sold everything they had, because the kingdom is worth more than anything else.

Câu 4. 4. What is the difference between the wise and the foolish virgins?

Đáp án: Five were wise and took oil for their lamps; five were foolish and took no oil, so they were not ready when the bridegroom came.

Câu 5. 5. What did the master do when he returned in the parable of the talents?

Đáp án: He rewarded the faithful servants, who had used his gifts well, and said, 'Well done, good and faithful servant.'
