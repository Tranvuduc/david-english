Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Present perfect: since/for, already/yet; present perfect vs past simple

Structure: have/has + past participle — I have read; she has studied; they have lived. The present perfect connects the past to NOW. Use it for a past action with a result that matters today, or for an experience in your life up to now.

Rule 1: since + a point in time; for + a length of time I have believed in Jesus since 2018 (a point: 2018). — We have studied the Sermon on the Mount for three weeks (a length: three weeks). Other points: since Monday, since I was a child, since last year. Other lengths: for five years, for a long time, for two days.

Rule 2: already (affirmative) and yet (negative and question) I have already read the Beatitudes twice this week. — She has already memorized Matthew 5:3. I have not memorized them yet. — Have you read the Sermon on the Mount yet? Already goes before the main verb; yet goes at the end.

Rule 3: Present perfect vs past simple — finished time or not? Use the past simple when the time is FINISHED: Jesus taught the Sermon on the Mount about two thousand years ago. — They visited the church last Sunday. Use the present perfect when NO finished time is given and the result matters now: I have read the Beatitudes twice this week. — Have you ever been to a Bible study on a mountain? Signal words for the past simple: yesterday, last week, in 2019, ago. Signal words for the present perfect: ever, never, already, yet, since, for, this week (the week is not finished).

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Word stress in longer words + sentence stress

Word Stress In English, every word with more than one syllable has one stressed syllable: it is louder, longer, and higher. The other syllables are weak and quick. Vietnamese speakers often stress every syllable equally, so English listeners hear the wrong word. Learn the stress of this lesson’s key words: BEATitudes, KINGdom, RIGHTeousness, MERciful, PERsecuted, tempTAtion, HYpocrite, disCIple.

Sentence Stress In a sentence, we stress the content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) and we say the grammar words (the, are, in, of) quickly and quietly. Say this sentence and stress only the words in bold: “Blessed are the poor in spirit.” Now try: “You are the salt of the earth.” and “Let your light shine before others.” Read each sentence aloud twice — once flat (wrong), once with the correct stress. Feel the rhythm!

Đoạn Kinh Thánh của bài: Matthew 5-7

Bài nghe

Nghe toàn bộ bài, rồi trả lời 5 câu hỏi bên dưới. Giọng đọc: Casper (người dẫn) và Aria (đối thoại).

Thời lượng: 2:29 · 372 từ · Xem transcript (TXT)

Câu hỏi nghe hiểu

Câu 1. 1. Where did Jesus sit to teach His disciples?

Đáp án: On a mountainside.

Câu 2. 2. What promises does Jesus give in the Beatitudes in the audio? Name three.

Đáp án: Any three of: the poor in spirit receive the kingdom of heaven; those who mourn will be comforted; the meek will inherit the earth; those who hunger and thirst for righteousness will be filled.

Câu 3. 3. What does 'meek' mean, according to the narrator?

Đáp án: Gentle and patient; not fighting for your own rights; trusting God to defend you.

Câu 4. 4. True or False: 'Blessed' means exactly the same as 'happy'.

Đáp án: False. Blessed is deeper than happy: it means God is pleased with you and you have His favor.

Câu 5. 5. Has the narrator already memorized the Beatitudes? How do you know?

Đáp án: No. He says, 'Not yet, but I have studied them for many years.'

Bài đọc

One day, large crowds followed Jesus up a mountainside. He sat down, His disciples came to Him, and He began to teach them. These words, in Matthew 5 to 7, are called the Sermon on the Mount, and they are the most famous teaching in the world. Jesus began with the Beatitudes, eight promises of blessing. He said that God blesses the poor in spirit, those who mourn, the meek, and those who hunger and thirst for righteousness. The world says that the rich, the strong, and the famous are blessed. Jesus says the opposite: God blesses the humble people who know they need Him.

Then Jesus gave His followers two pictures of their life in the world. They are the salt of the earth: just as salt gives flavor to food and keeps it from rotting, Christians should make life better for everyone around them. They are also the light of the world: just as a lamp on a hill cannot be hidden, a Christian life cannot be hidden. Their good works should shine before others so that people give glory to God. Jesus also taught His disciples how to pray. He gave them the Lord's Prayer, which begins with God's name and God's kingdom, and only then asks for daily bread, forgiveness, and protection from temptation. He warned them not to pray like hypocrites who love to be seen by others. Finally, Jesus taught about treasures. Earthly treasures are destroyed by moth and rust and stolen by thieves, but treasures in heaven last forever. 'For where your treasure is,' Jesus said, 'there your heart will be also.'

Nói

In pairs, discuss: (1) Which Beatitude is hardest for you to live, and why? (2) What does it mean to be “salt and light” in your family, your office, or your school? (3) Have you ever forgiven someone who hurt you? What happened? Use the present perfect (have/has) and at least 3 vocabulary words from Section 3. Then role-play the dialogue in Section 6, swapping roles.

Viết

Write 80–100 words: “Salt and Light in My Life.” Describe one way you have been salt or light this month. Use at least 3 present perfect sentences (with since, for, already, or yet) and 3 vocabulary words from Section 3.

Đọc hiểu

Câu 1. 1. According to Matthew 5:3-10, who receives the kingdom of heaven?

Đáp án: The poor in spirit.

Câu 2. 2. Why does Jesus call His followers 'the salt of the earth' and 'the light of the world'?

Đáp án: Because, like salt and light, Christians should make a real difference in the world: salt preserves and gives flavor, light shows the way and cannot be hidden.

Câu 3. 3. What does Jesus teach about prayer in Matthew 6:9-13?

Đáp án: He teaches His disciples to pray to 'Our Father in heaven', to honor God's name, to ask for His kingdom and will, for daily bread, for forgiveness, and for deliverance from temptation and evil.

Câu 4. 4. According to Matthew 6:19-21, where should we store our treasures, and why?

Đáp án: In heaven, because earthly treasures are destroyed by moth and rust and stolen by thieves, but heavenly treasures last forever; and where our treasure is, there our heart will be also.

Viết:

  1. Write 5 sentences about your own life using the present perfect: 2 with since, 2 with for, 1 with already or yet. Example: I have believed in Jesus since 2018.
  2. Write 3 sentences using 3 different vocabulary words from Section 3 (blessed, meek, merciful, treasure, forgive, light).
  3. Write the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3-6) in English from memory, then check your work against the Bible.
