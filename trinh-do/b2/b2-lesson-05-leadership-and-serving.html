Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Noun clauses (that / what / whether / if) and cleft sentences for emphasis (It is... who/that... / What... is...)

Noun clauses — a clause that works like a noun. That-clauses follow verbs of saying and believing: Jesus said that the greatest must be the servant. Paul teaches that an overseer must be above reproach. In speech, that can be dropped: Jesus said the greatest must be the servant.

Wh-clauses — what, who, why, how + clause. What matters most is character. I don't know why he wants to be a leader. Note the word order: a noun clause uses statement order, not question order. Say I don't know why he wants to be a leader, not I don't know why does he want to be a leader.

Whether and if clauses. I don't know whether he will be a good leader. Whether is more formal than if. Use whether after prepositions and before infinitives: the question of whether to choose him, I don't know whether to stay or go.

It-clefts for emphasis. Form: It + be + stressed part + who / that + rest of the sentence. It was Jesus who turned leadership upside down. The cleft puts a spotlight on one part of the sentence: not Paul, not Peter — Jesus. Use it when you want to correct someone or to underline the point of a sermon.

What-clefts for emphasis. Form: What + clause + be + stressed part. What matters most is character. What Paul requires is an overseer above reproach. Use clefts sparingly: one cleft in a paragraph gives it power; five clefts in a paragraph make it sound strange.

  • Jesus said that the greatest must be the servant.
  • What matters most is character, not talent.
  • It was Jesus who turned leadership upside down.
  • It is the servant who is truly great.
  • What Paul requires is an overseer above reproach.
  • I don't know whether he will be a good leader.
  • I wonder who the church will choose.
  • It is gentleness that shows a leader's heart.

Phát âm

Luyện phát âm theo bài — nghe audio và đọc to theo.

Word stress: suffixes -ity and -ation — the stress falls on the syllable just before the suffix

Rule 1 — words ending in -ity. The main stress falls on the syllable just before -ity: hu-MI-li-ty, a-BI-li-ty, re-spon-si-BI-li-ty, pos-si-BI-li-ty. The suffix -ity itself is never stressed.

Rule 2 — words ending in -ation. The stress falls on the syllable just before -ation: con-dem-NA-tion, ce-le-BRA-tion, re-ve-LA-tion, trans-for-MA-tion. Again, the suffix itself is never stressed.

Why the rule works. The suffix pulls the stress to the syllable next to it. Learn the word with its stress: say the whole family together. Humble, huMIlity. Able, aBIlity. Responsible, responsiBIlity. Condemn, condemNAtion. Transform, transforMAtion.

Practice. Read 1 Timothy 3:6 aloud and stress condemNAtion correctly: fall into the condemNAtion of the devil. Then read Romans 12:2: be transformed by the renewal of your mind — transformation: transforMAtion. Say each pair aloud, clapping on the stressed syllable.

Listen and repeat: huMIlity; aBIlity; responsiBIlity; possibility; condemNAtion; celeBRAtion; reveLAtion; transforMAtion

Câu 3. 3. What did Jesus say the greatest among believers must be?

Đáp án: The greatest must be the servant; the first must be slave of all.

Câu 4. 4. What does 1 Timothy 3:1 say about aspiring to be an overseer?

Đáp án: He desires a noble task.

Câu 5. 5. Name three qualities of an overseer from 1 Timothy 3:2-3.

Đáp án: Any three of: above reproach, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, not a drunkard, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money.

Câu 6. 6. Why must an overseer manage his household well?

Đáp án: If someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for God's church? (1 Timothy 3:5)

Bài đọc

Jesus called his disciples together and taught them a lesson about leadership that turned the world upside down. You know, he said, that those who are considered rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. That is how the world leads: from above, by power, by force. But it shall not be so among you. Whoever would be great among you must be your servant, and whoever would be first among you must be slave of all. For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many. Jesus did not only teach this; he lived it. He washed feet, he carried a cross, and he gave his life. The greatest leader in history was the greatest servant in history. Paul gives the same teaching in practical form when he lists the qualities of an overseer. The saying is trustworthy: If anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task. Therefore an overseer must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, not a drunkard, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money. Notice what is missing from the list: Paul does not ask for a great speaker, a rich man, or a popular leader. He asks for character. He must manage his own household well, with all dignity keeping his children submissive, for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for God's church? What happens at home shows what will happen in the church. He must not be a recent convert, or he may become puffed up with conceit and fall into the condemnation of the devil. Moreover, he must be well thought of by outsiders, so that he may not fall into disgrace, into a snare of the devil. So the church should choose servants, not kings. It is the servant who is truly great, and it is the gentle leader whom God blesses.
