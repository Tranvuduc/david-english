Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Discourse markers for argumentation (however, furthermore, on the other hand, in contrast, nevertheless) and concession clauses (although / even though / despite / in spite of)

Discourse markers — words that show how your ideas connect. However means but, in a formal way: Some laughed. However, some believed. Furthermore means and also, more strongly: Paul listened first. Furthermore, he proclaimed the truth. On the other hand gives the opposite view. In contrast gives the direct opposite. Nevertheless means despite that.

Position and punctuation. Markers usually come at the start of a sentence, followed by a comma: However, some believed. In the middle of a sentence, use two commas: Some, however, believed. Never write however without a comma after it at the start of a sentence.

Concession with although and even though. Although / even though + clause (with a verb): Although many people worshipped many gods, Paul showed them the one true God. Even though is stronger than although: it concedes something surprising. Even though some people laughed, Paul kept preaching.

Concession with despite and in spite of. Despite / in spite of + noun or verb-ing: Despite being alone, Paul spoke boldly. Do not mix the two patterns: Despite Paul was alone is wrong, and Although being alone is wrong. Although needs a verb; despite needs a noun or an -ing form.

Arguing gently. Use concession to agree before you disagree: Although I understand your view, I believe Christ is the only way. This is how Paul argued in Athens, and it is how Peter tells us to answer: with gentleness and respect.

  • Some laughed. However, some believed.
  • Paul listened first. Furthermore, he proclaimed the truth.
  • Although many people worshipped many gods, Paul showed them the one true God.
  • Despite being alone in Athens, Paul spoke boldly.
  • Even though some people laughed, Paul kept preaching.
  • My colleague is kind. On the other hand, she does not believe in Christ.
  • In contrast to the many gods of Athens, the true God made the world.
  • Although I understand your view, I believe Christ is the only way.

Thought groups and pausing in persuasive speech: chunking, one stressed word per group

English is spoken in chunks, not word by word. A thought group is a short chunk of words that belong together: Men of Athens, / I perceive that / in every way / you are very religious. We pause briefly between groups — not after every word, and not never at all.

Pauses show the structure. In a long verse, pause after the subject group and after each clause: The God who made the world / and everything in it / does not live in temples made by man. The listener can then follow the argument instead of drowning in words.

Stress one key word in each group, and let the other words go quiet: Men of ATHENS, / I perCEIVE that / in EVery way / you are very reLIGious. One strong word per group is enough. If you stress every word, nothing stands out.

Practice. Read Acts 17:27 aloud in groups: that they should seek God, / and perhaps feel their way toward him / and find him. Pause at each slash. Then read 1 Peter 3:15 the same way: Always being prepared / to make a defense / to anyone who asks you / for a reason for the hope that is in you.

Listen and repeat: Men of Athens, / I perceive that / in every way / you are very religious.; What therefore you worship as unknown, / this I proclaim to you.; The God who made the world / and everything in it / does not live in temples made by man.; In him we live / and move / and have our being.; Always being prepared / to make a defense / to anyone who asks you / for a reason for the hope that is in you.; Yet do it / with gentleness / and respect.

Bài đọc

Paul came to Athens, the most famous city of learning in the ancient world, and he found a city full of idols. The people of Athens worshipped many gods, and they were so careful not to miss one that they had built an altar with the inscription: To the unknown god. Paul stood in the midst of the Areopagus and began, not with an attack, but with respect: Men of Athens, I perceive that in every way you are very religious. For as I passed along and observed the objects of your worship, I found also an altar with this inscription. What therefore you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you. Paul found common ground, and then he proclaimed the truth. The God who made the world and everything in it, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not live in temples made by man, nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything. This God made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth, having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place, that they should seek God, and perhaps feel their way toward him and find him. Yet he is actually not far from each one of us, for in him we live and move and have our being. Paul even quoted their own poets: For we are indeed his offspring. Although many people in Athens worshipped many gods, Paul showed them the one true God. On the other hand, he never forced anyone. He reasoned, he persuaded, and he left the result to God. Peter gives the same rule to us: in your hearts honor Christ the Lord as holy, always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect. Be ready with a reason, and give it gently. That is evangelism in a pluralist world: respectful, clear, and gentle, leaving the result to God.
