Ngữ pháp

Giải thích và ví dụ từ chính bài học.

Perfect continuous tenses: present perfect continuous (have/has been + verb-ing) and past perfect continuous (had been + verb-ing)

Form. Present perfect continuous: have / has + been + verb-ing. Past perfect continuous: had + been + verb-ing. Example: I have been living here for five years. They had been waiting for the Messiah for many years before Jesus came.

Use 1 — an action that started in the past and is still going on. Present perfect continuous stresses the activity and its duration, not the finished result. Use it with for (a length of time) and since (a starting point). Example: She has been studying English since 2020. We have been praying for our city all year.

Use 2 — a recent activity with a visible result now. Example: I have been reading First Peter this week, so my Bible is full of notes. Your eyes are red — have you been crying? The continuous form explains the present situation.

Present perfect continuous or present perfect simple? Use the continuous when you want to stress how long the activity has lasted. Use the simple when you want to stress the completed result or the number of times. Compare: I have been writing letters all morning (the activity lasted all morning) with I have written three letters this morning (the finished result: three letters).

Use 3 — past perfect continuous: a longer action that was in progress before another past event. The longer action uses had been + verb-ing; the shorter, later event uses the past simple. Example: The fishermen had been working all night before Jesus came to them. The believers had been praying together when the Spirit came.

Watch out — stative verbs. Verbs that describe a state, not an action, are not used in continuous forms: know, believe, belong, love, hate, understand, need, want. Say I have known him for years, not I have been knowing him for years. Say We have belonged to this church since 2019, not We have been belonging.

Common mistake. Do not use the present continuous for a long duration that reaches the present. Wrong: I am living here for five years. Right: I have been living here for five years. The present continuous (I am living here now) describes only this moment; the perfect continuous connects the past to the present.

  • I have been living in Hanoi for five years.
  • She has been studying English since 2020.
  • They have been waiting for the bus for an hour.
  • We have been praying for our city all year.
  • I have been reading First Peter this week.
  • The fishermen had been working all night before Jesus came to them.
  • The believers had been praying together when the Spirit came.
  • He had been walking to the temple every day before he met Philip.

Phát âm

Stress shift in word families: how stress moves when a suffix is added, and noun-verb stress pairs

Rule 1 — suffixes move the stress. When you add a suffix such as -graphy, -itance or -ation, the main stress usually moves to the syllable just before the suffix. PHOtograph becomes phoTOgraphy. INherit becomes inHERitance. IMmigrant becomes immiGRAtion. Learn the whole word family together, not one word alone.

Rule 2 — noun and verb pairs. Many two-syllable words change their stress when they change their job. As a noun, the stress falls on the first syllable; as a verb, it falls on the second. CONduct is a noun (your conduct = your behaviour), conDUCT is a verb (to conduct a meeting). PREsent is a noun (a gift), preSENT is a verb (to present a report). REcord is a noun, reCORD is a verb. OBject is a noun (a thing), obJECT is a verb (to disagree).

Rule 3 — the vowel changes with the stress. An unstressed vowel is usually reduced to a short, weak sound. In PHOtograph the first vowel is full, but in phoTOgraphy the first vowel is weak. Do not pronounce every syllable with full strength: English rhythm is strong-weak, strong-weak.

Practice. Say each pair aloud, clapping on the stressed syllable: PHOtograph, phoTOgraphy; INherit, inHERitance; IMmigrant, immiGRAtion; CONduct (noun), conDUCT (verb); PREsent (noun), preSENT (verb); REcord (noun), reCORD (verb). Then read 1 Peter 2:12 aloud and stress the noun CONduct correctly: Keep your CONduct among the Gentiles honorable.

Listen and repeat: PHOtograph — phoTOgraphy; INherit — inHERitance; IMmigrant — immiGRAtion; CONduct (noun: behaviour) — conDUCT (verb: to lead); PREsent (noun: a gift) — preSENT (verb: to give); REcord (noun: a written account) — reCORD (verb: to write down); OBject (noun: a thing) — obJECT (verb: to disagree); PROduce (noun: farm products) — proDUCE (verb: to make)

Đáp án: A person who is living for a time in a place that is not home.

Đáp án: The Messiah — for many years.

Câu 6. 6. What does Peter ask believers to do in 1 Peter 2:11-12?

Bài đọc

Peter wrote his first letter to Christians who were scattered across Asia Minor, far from Jerusalem and far from home. They were strangers in the cities where they lived. They had no power, no protection, and no permanent place. Some of them were suffering because of their faith. Peter did not tell them to fight for a home on earth. Instead, he told them about a better home. God, in his great mercy, had caused them to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Their inheritance was imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for them. No thief could steal it, no fire could burn it, and no time could make it fade. While they waited, God himself was guarding them by his power through faith. Their trials were real, and Peter did not pretend otherwise. He said their faith was being tested like gold in fire, and tested faith is more precious than gold. Peter also told them who they were: a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's own possession. God had called them out of darkness into his marvelous light so that they would proclaim his excellencies. And because they belonged to God, they were to live differently. As sojourners and exiles, they were to abstain from the passions of the flesh, which wage war against the soul, and to keep their conduct honorable among unbelievers, so that others would see their good deeds and glorify God. Peter's message is our message too. We are living far from our true home, but we are not homeless. Our inheritance is waiting, and our hope is alive.

Nói

Roleplay in pairs (3 minutes): Student A tells Student B about a time when you felt like a stranger in a new place (a new city, a new school, a new country). Student B asks two questions about it. Use at least 4 perfect continuous sentences (I have been living... / We had been waiting...) and 5 vocabulary words from Section 3. Practise the stress-shift pairs: photograph / photography, inherit / inheritance, CONduct (noun) / conDUCT (verb).

Viết

Write a short essay (120-150 words) titled 'My Life as a Sojourner'. Describe where you live now, how long you have been living there, what you have been learning, and what hope you hold for the future. Use at least 4 perfect continuous sentences (present perfect continuous and past perfect continuous) and 5 vocabulary words from Section 3.

Đọc hiểu

Câu 1. 1. To whom did Peter write, and where were they living?

Đáp án: To Christians scattered across Asia Minor, living far from home as strangers in the cities where they lived.

Câu 2. 2. What three words describe the believers' inheritance in 1 Peter 1:4?

Đáp án: Imperishable, undefiled, and unfading.

Câu 3. 3. Who guards the believers while they wait, and how?

Đáp án: God guards them by his power through faith (1 Peter 1:5).

Câu 4. 4. What four things does Peter call believers in 1 Peter 2:9?

Đáp án: A chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, and a people for God's own possession.

Câu 5. 5. Why must believers keep their conduct honorable among unbelievers?

Đáp án: So that when others speak against them as evildoers, they may see their good deeds and glorify God on the day of visitation (1 Peter 2:12).
